TrafficSignBench

High driving scores can hide illegal behaviour.

We introduce a large-scale closed-loop benchmark that jointly combines a broad traffic-sign taxonomy, automatic rule checkers, and rule-targeted scenarios on real road maps — to test whether autonomous-driving planners actually obey traffic signs.

  • 20.6K closed-loop scenes
  • 34 traffic signs
  • Real maps of Moscow

The benchmark

What is inside TrafficSignBench?

Three ingredients turn a traffic sign into a measurable closed-loop test: an executable rule for every sign, real road geometry to place it in, and semantics that carry over to other countries.

01 Traffic signs 02 Real maps 03 Generalizability
01

34 traffic signs, sorted by what they ask of the driver

Every sign is a simulator object with its own executable rule checker. The checker watches the closed-loop rollout and flags a violation the moment the ego vehicle breaks the rule — no human labelling required.

02

Built on the real road network of Moscow

We crop 26,020 candidate locations from the OpenStreetMap road network, convert them with SUMO, and place the sign exactly where its rule applies. Every crop becomes an executable closed-loop test.

03

Rule semantics generalize internationally

The 34 implemented signs have direct semantic equivalents in other Vienna-Convention countries — and beyond it. A planner that passes TrafficSignBench is tested on rules that drivers meet across the world.

Evaluation

Current planners fall short on rule compliance

SCD — Sign-Compliant Destination — counts an episode as a success only if the ego vehicle obeys the sign and reaches its goal. 5,800 held-out episodes across 29 scenario types.

Failure cases from the paper CaRL, PlanT-2, and PPO still break yield, crosswalk, and mandatory-direction rules
Held-out test split 2.9–9.0%

SCD across standard planners. Even the strongest one obeys the sign and reaches the goal in fewer than one episode in ten.
